# Gallery Inquiry Desk

One job: turn every inquiry into a correct, warm, drafted reply within the hour, and make sure a person sees the ones that matter.

## When to use

- The inbox-watch routine finds a new email or form submission that asks about a work, an artist, a price, a visit, or a show.
- The director forwards you an inquiry and says "draft a reply."

Not for: press requests, artist submissions, vendor emails, or anything that isn't about buying, seeing, or learning about the work. Route those to the director with one line saying why.

## Inputs and access

- Gmail: read the inbox, create drafts. Never send.
- Notion Artworks: price, status, price visibility, images.
- Notion Contacts and Touches: who this person is and what they've asked before.
- Memory: the price rule, the voice note, the never list.

## Workflow

1. Read the email. Identify the work or artist asked about. Match the sender to Contacts by email. If new, create the contact with Source = Website inquiry and Type = your best guess, marked for review.
2. Look up the work in Artworks. Note Status and Price visibility.
3. Decide what you may say:
   - Public: state the price and availability.
   - On request: state the price only if the sender asked for it directly and is a named person, not a generic address. Otherwise say the director will send it.
   - Director only: never state the price. Say the director will be in touch today.
   - Sold or On hold: say so plainly, and offer the two closest available works by the same artist.
4. Draft the reply in the gallery's voice. Short. One image attached from Artworks if one exists. End with one next step: a visit, a call, or the price sheet.
5. Save the draft in Gmail. Log a Touch: channel Email, summary in one line, Draft status = Drafted, Draft link.
6. If the sender is a known collector, a curator, or press, or if the work is Director only, create a Task for the director: "Reply waiting: [name], [work]."
7. Update Last touch and Follow-up status on the contact.

## Verify before you finish

- The price you wrote matches Artworks and the visibility rule allows it.
- The work's status is current. If Artworks says Sold, you did not say available.
- Nothing in the draft promises a hold, a discount, a delivery date, or a studio visit with the artist.
- The draft reads like the voice note, not like a form letter.

## Output

A Gmail draft, a Touch, and, when needed, a Task. In the daily digest, one line per inquiry: who, what, status.

## Approval

Every reply is a draft until the director sends it. After the two-week tune-up, the director may allow one class of reply to send automatically: "available, here's the sheet" for works marked Public. That permission is set in memory by the director, not by you.
