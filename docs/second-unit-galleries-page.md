## The part of the job nobody sees

You run a gallery with two people, or one, or you on a Tuesday with the installer coming at noon.

An inquiry comes in during install week and sits for three days. The sign-in book from Saturday's opening is still on the desk on Thursday, and the people in it have cooled. A consignment term ends and you find out when the artist asks for the work back. The press release gets written at midnight because that's the only hour nobody needs you.

Everything that keeps the lights on runs through you: the sale, the shipping quote, the artist who wants an update, the collector who wants a price, the invoice nobody chased. Nothing is hard. There's too much of it, and all of it waits for the same person.

A reply that comes three days late costs you the sale. The collector was serious on Saturday. By Wednesday they bought something else.

## Why it's called Second Unit

On a film set, the second unit shoots everything that doesn't need the director: the establishing shots, the inserts, the driving scenes. The director keeps the scenes that need them.

Same deal here. The agents take the replies, the follow-ups, and the consignment dates. You keep the conversations that sell the work.

## Three jobs, one gallery

**Inquiry Desk.** Every email and website inquiry gets a drafted reply within the hour, in your voice, with the right image attached. It knows which prices are public, which are on request, and which are yours alone to discuss. Serious collectors get flagged to you the same day.

**Collector Follow-up.** Photograph the sign-in book the morning after the opening and upload it. By the time you've had coffee, every name is in your contact list, every visit is logged, and every person has a personal thank-you waiting in your drafts with an image of what they looked at. Nobody who signs the book goes quiet.

**Consignment Tracker.** Drop the agreement in a folder. The agent reads the works, the term, the split, and the insurance value, and 30 days before the term ends it drafts the renewal or the return note. You stop finding out from the artist.

Want a different job first? Press and preview kits, fair prep, artist updates, and exhibition checklists are on the menu. Ask on the call.

## How it works

1. **Assessment.** A 45-minute call. We find the one thing that's eating your week and match it to a job.
2. **Install.** You sign up for Grok Bot and Notion on your own accounts. In one screen share we install the agents, connect your email and calendar from your side, and run the interview that teaches them your voice, your artists, and your rules.
3. **Two weeks of tuning.** We read the drafts with you every few days and tighten the agent until most go out with no edits. Then a monthly check-in, and the next job when you want it.

Most galleries are running within a week of the assessment.

## What it won't do

- It won't send anything, publish anything, or change a price, an owner, or a provenance record without your yes.
- It won't replace your registrar, your assistant, or you. One agent, one job, with a person approving the output.
- It won't hold your logins. Your accounts, your data, your collectors. If we part ways, the agents stay with you.
- It won't do cold outreach. It follows up with people who walked into your gallery and signed their name.
